- # Order Flow Absorption as Trend Termination
- ## TL;DR
	- Absorption is when heavy volume and delta trade at a level but price doesn't move — a passive buyer or seller is sitting on limits and absorbing everything. Per skew, this is the number-one ender of trends. The participant has decided the move has gone far enough and is deploying capital or unloading inventory against it.
- ### Context
	- Most retail traders look for aggressive market activity to signal exhaustion (climactic volume, blow-off candles). The more reliable signal is the opposite — heavy *passive* activity with no price progress. This is invisible on a regular candlestick chart but obvious on footprint.
- ## Core Concept
	- A trend continues while aggressive participants meet weak passive resistance. The moment a large passive participant decides to defend a level, market orders hit their limits and produce volume and delta — but price stalls because the limits keep refilling. Once the aggressive side exhausts, price reverses, sometimes sharply.
- ## Mechanics
	- **Look for the divergence.** Price stalls or grinds sideways while CVD continues moving up (or down). Volume is high but price progress is zero — that's flow being absorbed.
	- **Confirm on footprint.** Heavy delta prints at the same price levels with no breakout. Often the passive prints come in consistent same-size chunks (e.g. repeated +2M or −2M) — that's a single player scaling on limits.
	- **Wait for momentum death.** CVD divergence + stalled price = aggressive side running out of fuel. Reversal often follows within minutes.
- ## Entry / Trigger
	- Counter-trend entry: heavy passive absorption at a higher-timeframe level, CVD divergence vs price, and momentum dies on the lower timeframe. Enter against the prior trend with stops just past the absorption zone.
- ## Risk Management
	- Stops just beyond the absorption price — if price breaks through, the absorbing participant has been overrun and the trend is intact. Best used as a scalp or short-swing tool unless the absorption coincides with a higher-timeframe end-of-trend setup.
- ## Examples
	- The interview frames this as "the number one ender of trends" — large participants deciding a move is too extended (too expensive or too discounted), then starting to deploy capital or sell inventory passively. The visible result is sharp reversals after a level held for what seemed like no reason.
- ## Caveats & Edge Cases
	- Most useful as a scalp signal. For higher-timeframe swing trades, absorption matters mainly when it appears at the end of an extended trend — mid-trend absorption can be temporary and price often continues.
	- Requires order-flow tools (footprint, sized CVD). Standard candles will not show this — by the time the reversal candle prints, you've missed the entry.
