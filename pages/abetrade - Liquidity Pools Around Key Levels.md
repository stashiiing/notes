- # abetrade - Liquidity Pools Around Key Levels
- ## TL;DR
	- Significant resting orders sit at predictable price levels: prior day/week highs and lows, major swing points, round numbers, and prices highlighted by financial media. Price is drawn to these levels because two types of orders cluster there, stops from traders already in positions, and breakout orders from traders anticipating continuation. Recognising these pools helps traders anticipate probes and position accordingly rather than reacting to them.
- ### Context
	- Liquidity has become a heavily marketed buzzword, with many course sellers framing it as "large players hunting retail stop losses." The reality is more mechanical: resting orders naturally accumulate at obvious price levels, and price moves toward those orders because that is where the volume exists to fill larger participants. Understanding this mechanistically is more useful than narrative-driven explanations.
- ## Core Concept
	- Liquidity = resting limit orders in the order book waiting to be filled. The DOM (Depth of Market) displays this directly.
	- Liquidity concentrates at: prior session highs/lows (daily, weekly), key higher timeframe swing points, round numbers, and price levels publicly discussed by financial media.
	- Two types of orders sit below support and above resistance simultaneously:
		- Stop-loss orders from traders long (below support) or short (above resistance).
		- Breakout entry orders from traders anticipating continuation through the level.
	- When price probes these levels, it triggers both sets of orders, creating a spike in participation. This spike is often visible on a footprint chart as a burst of aggressive order flow.
	- After the probe, one of two outcomes follows: the liquidity is absorbed (reversal) or the breakout is genuine (continuation).
- ## Entry / Trigger
	- Approaching a known liquidity pool (prior swing high/low, round number): heighten attention rather than acting immediately, the probe itself is not the signal.
	- Look for evidence of absorption after the probe: price rejecting back through the level, structural shift on the lower timeframe, or footprint-visible absorption of aggressive orders by passive participants.
- ## Caveats & Edge Cases
	- Not all liquidity probes reverse. Sometimes the breakout through the pool is genuine and continuation follows. The probe alone is not a trading signal, it is a context signal to pay attention.
	- The concept is fractal: daily double-bottom probes mirror intraday spike-and-recover patterns. The same behaviour happens across all timeframes.
	- Trying to assign intentionality ("large players hunting stops") is pointless and unverifiable. Focus on the mechanical reality: participation spikes at these levels, and the resulting order flow determines direction.
