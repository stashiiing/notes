- # Start Small, Expand Concentrically
- ## TL;DR
	- The path to building a large monopoly business runs through a small market, not a large one. Dominate a niche completely first, achieve monopoly-level penetration, then expand into adjacent markets. Starting with a huge addressable market is typically evidence that the problem has been framed incorrectly and that there will be too much competition to build a durable position.
- ### Context
	- Counterintuitively, the most valuable technology businesses almost all started by serving markets so small that observers dismissed them as unimportant. Business school analysis of these early stages routinely concludes the market is too small to matter, which misses the point entirely. Small market plus high penetration plus expansion path is the correct model.
- ## Core Concept
	- You need a large share of a market to have monopoly power. The only reliable way to achieve a large share is to start in a market small enough to dominate completely, then expand. The small starting market is not a limitation; it is the foundation. The critical question is not how big the initial market is, but whether there is a credible path of concentric expansion from it.
- ## Mechanics
	- Step one: identify a market small enough that you can reach dominant penetration quickly. The market should be so small that meaningful competitors do not bother entering it.
	- Step two: build a product that is significantly better than anything else available in that specific market. Penetration at 25 to 30 percent within a few months is an indicator you have found the right starting point.
	- Step three: expand from that core into adjacent markets that share users, infrastructure, or demand characteristics. Each expansion should replicate the dominance logic at a slightly larger scale.
	- Beware: entering a large existing market on day one is almost always a mistake. It typically means you have not defined your category correctly, and it guarantees intense competition before you have established any durable advantage.
- ## Examples
	- Amazon started as a bookstore with every book in the world, dominating that specific format of retail before expanding into all e-commerce and beyond.
	- eBay started with Pez dispenser auctions, moved to Beanie Babies, and gradually expanded to all auction categories.
	- PayPal targeted eBay power sellers, approximately 20,000 people in late 1999. The market looked trivially small. Within two to three months PayPal had 25 to 30 percent penetration, generating the brand recognition and network density needed to expand.
	- Facebook launched to 10,000 Harvard students. It reached 60 percent market share in ten days, an extremely strong signal before expanding to other universities and eventually the general population.
	- Clean-tech companies in the 2005 to 2008 period consistently made the opposite mistake: every pitch deck opened with "we are in the energy market, a multi-trillion-dollar industry." Being a minnow in a vast ocean meant facing unlimited competition with no clear path to dominance.
- ## Caveats & Edge Cases
	- The concentric expansion path must be real, not theoretical. A small market with no adjacent expansion opportunity is simply a small business, not a monopoly in waiting. The question is whether the initial market is a genuine beachhead or a dead end.
