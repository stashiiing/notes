- # Characteristics of a Monopoly Business
- ## TL;DR
	- There is no single formula for building a monopoly, but durable ones tend to share four characteristics: proprietary technology with a step-change advantage, network effects, economies of scale, and brand. These features both win a market at a moment in time and, crucially, defend that position over time. Thinking about which of these your business has, and whether they compound or erode with time, is the core of competitive strategy.
- ### Context
	- Identifying that you want a monopoly is not enough. Understanding which structural features create and sustain monopoly power allows founders and investors to evaluate whether a given business can actually achieve and defend one. Each characteristic has a time dimension: some strengthen over time, some erode.
- ## Core Concept
	- Monopoly power is built and maintained through some combination of four structural features. Not all four are required, and different businesses rely on different combinations. Software is unusual in that it naturally combines strong economies of scale with the potential for proprietary technology and network effects simultaneously.
- ## Mechanics
	- **Proprietary technology**: a rule of thumb is that your technology should be at least ten times better than the next best alternative on some meaningful dimension. This is the threshold at which you can reliably capture a market rather than merely compete in one. If you invent something genuinely new with no prior equivalent, the improvement is effectively infinite. Amazon had more than ten times as many books as any physical retailer. PayPal settled transactions more than ten times faster than cheques clearing through the banking system. The iPhone was the first smartphone that actually worked.
	- **Network effects**: a product becomes more valuable to each user as total users increase. Network effects are difficult to initiate because the product has low value to early adopters. Once established, they become highly durable and often grow stronger as the network scales. Every successful social platform and payments network relies on this.
	- **Economies of scale**: high fixed costs combined with near-zero marginal costs produce a structural advantage that grows as the business scales. The unit cost falls with every additional user or transaction, making it progressively harder for smaller competitors to match your price or margin. Software is the extreme case: marginal cost is effectively zero, so once a product works, scaling is almost pure profit expansion.
	- **Brand**: a form of value that is lodged in users' minds and creates preference that is not purely rational or price-driven. Thiel acknowledges this is the least well-understood of the four characteristics and the only one he does not invest in as a standalone proposition.
- ## Caveats & Edge Cases
	- Proprietary technology is inherently unstable over time. Disk drive manufacturers in the 1980s had technically superior products that were rendered obsolete every two years by competitors. Having a ten-times-better product today does not protect you unless you can sustain the pace of improvement faster than competitors can catch up. The question is not just whether you have proprietary technology now, but whether your technology will still be the leading one in ten years.
	- Network effects and economies of scale tend to be more durable than technology leads alone.
