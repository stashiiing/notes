- # Arbitrage as a Business
- ## TL;DR
	- Approaching trading through a business lens means asking whether a strategy is repeatable, scalable, and verifiable rather than whether a recent trade felt skilful. Arbitrage and structural trades meet this test because the edge is observable before entry. Directional speculation often does not, because luck and skill are hard to separate after the fact.
- ### Context
	- Many traders, particularly in crypto, measure success by the largest win rather than the consistency of process. This leads to oversizing directional bets and undersizing or ignoring structural opportunities. The business framing corrects this by treating capital as inventory and edge as a product that must be reliably manufactured.
- ## Core Concept
	- A trade qualifies as a business-style trade when you can articulate the mechanism that produces the return, verify it exists before entry, and bet confidently large because the odds are demonstrably in your favour. Directional trades rarely meet this bar because even a successful run cannot confirm that the edge is repeatable rather than coincidental.
- ## Mechanics
	- Evaluate any trade by asking: is this a service I am providing to the market (liquidity, convergence, carry) in exchange for a known or estimable payment? If yes, it is a business trade.
	- Size aggressively on business trades because the mechanism is known. larpcapitalwc put his entire available capital into the first Binance AUD/USDT arbitrage he executed because the edge was unambiguous.
	- On directional trades, ask honestly: if this works, does it prove I have a repeatable process, or could I have been lucky? A 100% gain on a meme coin does not answer this question.
	- Use the expected value framework relative to your opportunity set. A trader with $1M who can generate 50% annually should not take a salaried job paying $100k. A trader with $100k generating 30% ($30k) should question whether that capital and time are better deployed elsewhere.
	- Compound within the business: one structural trade often reveals the next adjacent one rather than requiring a leap to an unrelated strategy.
- ## Caveats & Edge Cases
	- The framework does not rule out directional trading entirely. If you can demonstrate a statistical edge through autocorrelation, momentum, or Hurst exponent analysis, that too is a business trade.
	- Small capital bases face a ceiling with structural trades because many arbitrages lose their edge as size increases. At small scale, the highest-EV activity may be earning income rather than trading at all.
	- Business trades can still go wrong if convergence fails or if a counterparty exploits a structural weakness you did not anticipate. The business framing reduces but does not eliminate risk.
