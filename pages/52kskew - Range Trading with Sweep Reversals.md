- # Range Trading with Sweep Reversals
- ## TL;DR
	- During ranges, define the boundaries from where price has reacted multiple times. Most of the range is just chop. The high-EV moments are the "blowouts" — price extends sharply past a range boundary, sweeps all the stops sitting just outside, then reverses in literally 5 minutes or so. These are some of the best fade entries available because the stop-runs are mechanical and the reversal is immediate.
- ### Context
	- Trend traders struggle in ranges because their entire framework requires direction. But ranges aren't dead time — they have a repeating pattern (boundary tag, occasional blowout, sweep, reverse). Recognising this pattern means you have an active strategy in a regime that would otherwise burn through your trend setups.
- ## Core Concept
	- A range has visible boundaries where price reacts. Most touches produce normal reversals. But every range contains a few extension events where price *blows past* the boundary to sweep the cluster of stops sitting just outside it. Those stops are the liquidity. Once they're harvested, price snaps back into the range. The blowout is the trap; the snap-back is the trade.
- ## Mechanics
	- **Define the range.** Find horizontal levels where price has reacted multiple times — that's your range.
	- **Wait for the blowout.** Price extends past the boundary in a sharp move, further than typical range touches.
	- **Look for stop-cluster targeting.** The extension is unusually thin and clearly aimed at the obvious stop area.
	- **Wait for the reversal.** "Then reverses in like literally 5 minutes or something like that, right? Sharp moves like that — really sharp moves. Some of the best shorts like ever, or like longs."
	- **Fade.** Enter against the blowout direction once the reversal starts. Stops just past the sweep extreme.
- ## Entry / Trigger
	- Sharp boundary extension into a known stop cluster + immediate momentum reversal (price snaps back through the boundary). Enter on the snap-back, not on the extension itself.
- ## Risk Management
	- Stops sit just past the sweep extreme — if price extends further with continued momentum, the move isn't a sweep, it's a break. Size larger than a trend trade if the range has been respected for some time, because the reversal is mechanical and tight.
- ## Examples
	- Skew notes that since the October 2025 wipeout, this setup has been particularly effective on the short side — the spooking of long-side participants made long-side blowouts less common, but he sees the long-side response returning recently with lower-timeframe lows producing the same mechanical buyer.
- ## Caveats & Edge Cases
	- Regime-dependent. Works in clear ranges; fails when the range breaks structurally (the "blowout" turns out to be a real breakout).
	- Best confluence: blowouts that occur during illiquid hours or right around scheduled events — those are the highest-probability stop-runs.
	- Don't try this in a trending market — a "sweep" of a level in a trending market is often just trend continuation with a wick.
