- # Trader Network Building
- ## TL;DR
	- A small network of traders who think similarly is one of the highest-leverage investments an independent trader can make. A single hour-long phone call can compress a week of solo investigation into a quick yes or no. The network also catches sizing errors before they become large losses, and surfaces the next trade as an organic conversation rather than a cold search.
- ### Context
	- Independent trading is isolating and the feedback loops are slow. A bad idea can consume weeks before it is clearly unprofitable. A good idea can be missed because you lack context in an adjacent domain. A network of peers with overlapping methods provides fast feedback on both. It also makes sharing ideas rational: because there are only a limited number of ways to extract money from markets, sharing a structural trade with a trusted peer rarely destroys the edge.
- ## Core Concept
	- The value of a trading network is proportional to the overlap between members' analytical methods. A quant trader gains little from a breakout trader's perspective, but a great deal from another quant or arbitrageur. The network is built by publicly sharing your process on Twitter or equivalent, which attracts people working on similar problems and leads to group chats and collaborations over time.
- ## Mechanics
	- Post publicly about your trading process, questions, and observations. Do not wait until you are successful. The act of posting attracts people at similar stages.
	- Prioritise depth over breadth. A small number of traders you can call on the phone is worth more than a large number of acquaintances.
	- Use the network for two specific purposes: stress-testing a new idea before committing capital, and confirming that a trade going against you is still structurally valid rather than broken.
	- Share ideas freely within the network when the edge is structural. There are a limited number of ways to extract money from markets, and one person's participation rarely destroys a structural trade.
	- Engage with people who have domain knowledge adjacent to your current trade. larpcapitalwc called Liquidity Goblin specifically because the new trade he was investigating fell into a domain where Goblin had deep knowledge.
- ## Caveats & Edge Cases
	- Network validation is not a substitute for your own analysis. larpcapitalwc lost money on a trade that peers initially confirmed looked interesting. Ultimately the conviction and sizing decision remain yours.
	- The network works best when members are candid rather than polite. A friend who tells you a trade is fine when they have doubts is worse than no second opinion.
	- Building the network takes time and requires genuine contribution. Asking for alpha without sharing your own thinking is a poor exchange that will not sustain good relationships.
