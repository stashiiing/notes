- # abetrade - Efficient vs Inefficient Price Moves
- ## TL;DR
	- Markets are either in an efficient state (slow, choppy grind through dense liquidity) or an inefficient state (sharp, fast move through a liquidity void). Efficient moves exhaust the resting orders in their path; when an inefficient breakout eventually follows, the previously traversed efficient channel offers little support on the way back, making the reversal fast and deep.
- ### Context
	- Traders often describe moves as "choppy" or "fast" without a structural explanation for why. The efficient/inefficient framework ties price behaviour to the underlying liquidity landscape, explaining why some reversals are shallow and slow while others are steep and rapid. It also explains why sharp breakouts above consolidations often collapse back quickly, the channel below lacked liquidity to support the move.
- ## Core Concept
	- Efficient move: price moves slowly and methodically, testing each level of resting liquidity in sequence. The order book is full; every resting order gets traded through. This is visible as a grinding, overlapping candle structure.
	- Inefficient move: price skips rapidly through a zone with few resting orders. Little to no friction. This appears as a sharp, clean leg with minimal wicks or pullbacks.
	- The relationship between the two: an efficient channel being traversed slowly means most liquidity within it has been absorbed. When an inefficient breakout occurs above (or below) this channel, any reversal back into it will move quickly, there are few resting orders to slow the descent because they were already consumed during the efficient phase.
- ## Mechanics
	- Identify the current price movement type: is price grinding through levels (efficient) or gapping cleanly (inefficient)?
	- During an efficient grind toward a key level, prepare for the possibility of an inefficient breakout followed by sharp reversal, the channel below is now thin.
	- When an inefficient move extends far from the prior efficient channel, the efficient zone below becomes a vacuum, a fast-path target for any reversal.
	- Volume profile is a complementary tool: LVNs (Low Volume Nodes) correspond to inefficient move zones; HVNs correspond to efficient zones.
- ## Entry / Trigger
	- After an inefficient spike above a key level (e.g., swing high) that fails and reverses, the subsequent move back through the efficient channel below is likely to be fast. This creates a momentum entry or target extension opportunity.
- ## Examples
	- Example from the article: price grinds efficiently toward a monthly swing high, then spikes inefficiently above it. Passive sellers absorb aggressive buyers at the high. With little resting support in the efficient channel below, price collapses rapidly toward the swing low.
	- Gold chart example: efficient channel formation followed by sharp inefficient spike up and equally sharp reversal to the next inefficient level below, demonstrating the symmetry of the framework across different markets.
- ## Caveats & Edge Cases
	- Not every inefficient spike reverses. Genuine breakouts also appear as sharp, clean moves, the probe structure and order flow context (footprint, delta) are needed to distinguish absorbed breakouts from genuine ones.
	- The framework is most useful after the fact as a target-setting tool (how far might the reversal go?) rather than as a standalone entry signal.
