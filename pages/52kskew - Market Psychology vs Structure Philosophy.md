- # Market Psychology vs Structure Philosophy
- ## TL;DR
	- "Buy when others are fearful" sounds clean but blows up accounts in practice. Sentiment and momentum are correlated — extreme fear on a 10% red day reflects real negative momentum that probably has more downside. Extreme euphoria reflects real positive momentum still pushing higher. You can't trade sentiment alone; you need it to coincide with structural exhaustion. The reliable signal is "degenerate positioning into a level the market can't break through" — that pattern has flipped trends every time skew has seen it in five years.
- ### Context
	- Beginners read about Wall Street Cheat Sheet psychology and assume they can trade the curve directly. They long during "denial" or short during "euphoria" and get destroyed because they're trading sentiment without structure. The point of this page is to make that mistake explicit and lay out what actually works.
- ## Core Concept
	- Sentiment is not a leading indicator on its own. It's a *coincident* indicator with momentum — when sentiment is most extreme, momentum is most extreme, which means the move usually has fuel left. The extremity becomes actionable only when paired with structural exhaustion: a level that can't break despite all the positioning piling in, or a higher-timeframe structure already shifted.
- ## Mechanics
	- **Sentiment ≈ momentum.** Extreme bearish sentiment on a red day = strong downside momentum. Extreme bullish sentiment = strong upside momentum. They move together, not opposite.
	- **The structure overlay.** Before acting on extreme sentiment, check the higher-timeframe chart. If the trend hasn't already structurally broken, extreme sentiment is just trend continuation with extra noise.
	- **The actionable pattern — failed breakout under degenerate positioning.** When sentiment-driven positioning is enormous and the market still can't push through a level, that combination has reversed every time skew has seen it in five years. This is the signal, not raw sentiment.
	- **Find the catalyst.** Major reversals are usually triggered by something specific — the Coinbase IPO marked the 2021 top; CME futures launch marked the 2017 top; "exchanges going down" tends to coincide. Sentiment extremes are the symptom, but the catalyst is the timestamp.
	- **Apply at the right structural location.** Bottoms produce buying opportunities when extreme sentiment coincides with a major higher-timeframe level (e.g. 2021 highs as support, 1W 200 EMA holding). Earlier in the year, when structure was leaning down, "pessimism" wasn't a buy signal — it was just trend continuation, and the trade was shorting overvalued alts instead.

- ## Examples
	- 2017 top: CME futures launch + euphoria + exchanges going down — structure-driven exhaustion at a sentiment extreme.
	- 2021 top: Coinbase IPO + euphoria — same pattern, different catalyst.
	- "Earlier in the year" (2025) pessimism: structure was leaning down, so pessimism wasn't a buy — it was a signal to short overvalued alts. The actual buying opportunity came at the major HTF level, not at the sentiment extreme.
- ## Caveats & Edge Cases
	- Roller-coaster meme charts (sentiment getting "smaller and smaller" through each cycle) capture something real about diminishing returns but aren't a tradeable model on their own.
	- Sentiment is most useful as a *filter* — if all your structural signals say "reverse" and sentiment is also extreme, that's confirmation. If structure says nothing yet, sentiment alone is noise.
